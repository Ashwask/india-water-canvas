# Public artefacts inventory

A structured read of what's publicly available, with an honest usage assessment. The headline observation: roughly 200-500 substantial public water artefacts exist in India. Maybe 10-20 are heavily used in operational decisions. Maybe 50-100 reach policy + research circles. The remainder sit underused.

The artefact-to-decision conversion rate is brutally low. India has data abundance and decision starvation.

## Tier 1 — Government dashboards (live)

- **India-WRIS** (indiawris.gov.in) — flagship aggregator. Reservoir, GW, basin, water quality. Modest traffic.
- **CGWB portal** (cgwb.gov.in) — well data, aquifer maps, GEC reports.
- **CWC eFlows + flood forecast** (cwc.gov.in) — real-time at major stations.
- **IMD Mausam** (mausam.imd.gov.in) — rainfall, forecasts, agromet.
- **Bhuvan** (bhuvan.nrsc.gov.in) — ISRO satellite portal: water bodies, drought, wetlands, snow.
- **JJM dashboard** (ejalshakti.gov.in/jjmreport) — tap connection coverage, real-time, daily.
- **Atal Bhujal dashboard** — village water budgets in 8,000+ GPs across 7 states.
- **NMCG portal** — Ganga monitoring.
- **CPCB water quality portal** — partial.
- **State portals** — Telangana Water Authority, KSPCB (Karnataka), MPCB (Maharashtra), TNPCB (Tamil Nadu), DPCC (Delhi). Uneven quality.
- **National Hydrology Project** (World Bank-supported) — surface water modernization layer.

**Usage read:** IMD products are heavily used (agriculture + public). CWC for dam ops is heavily used internally. Bhuvan satellite is heavily used by researchers. India-WRIS sees modest traffic. CPCB portal sees low public traffic. JJM dashboard is high politically (tap-coverage celebration), low analytically.

## Tier 2 — Government reports (periodic)

- **CWC Water Yearbook** — annual.
- **CGWB Ground Water Yearbook + GEC report** — annual + every 2-3 years (latest 2023).
- **CPCB State of Water Quality** — annual, with 6-12 month publication lag.
- **NITI Aayog Composite Water Management Index** (CWMI) — published 2018, 2019. **Quietly discontinued.** This is the diagnostic case: when ranking embarrassed states, the index disappeared.
- **5th + 6th India Census of Minor Irrigation** — water source data.
- **NFHS-5 (2019-21)** water + sanitation modules.
- **NSSO drinking water + sanitation surveys.**
- **FSI India State of Forest Report** — biennial, latest 2023.
- **CAG audit reports** on water schemes (Namami Gange, JJM — both flagged misreporting).
- **Standing Committee on Water Resources** reports — Lok Sabha.
- **Working Group reports** for Five Year Plans (water).
- **12th + 13th Finance Commission** state water allocations.

**Usage read:** Yearbooks read by policy + research circles. Census/NFHS heavily used. NITI's discontinued CWMI is the telling case.

## Tier 3 — International datasets (free)

- **GRACE / GRACE-FO** (NASA gravimetry). Basin-scale GW. Showed Northwest India losing ~17 km³/year — the most striking single piece of Indian water data ever produced. Indian agencies don't operationalize it.
- **HydroSHEDS** — global river network, basin boundaries. Free.
- **Sentinel / Landsat / MODIS** — satellite imagery, free, well-used by researchers.
- **CHIRPS, ERA5** — rainfall reanalysis.
- **WRI Aqueduct** — water risk maps. Heavily used by global corporates with India operations.
- **CMIP6 downscaled** — climate-water projections.
- **ICIMOD HKH portals** — Himalayan glaciers + basins.
- **Pacific Institute, World Bank Water Data, UNESCO IHE** — comparative resources.
- **SWOT** (NASA Surface Water and Ocean Topography) — recent altimetry mission.

**Usage read:** Indian academic researchers use these heavily. Indian agencies barely. The asymmetry is striking — Indian water knowledge often lives offshore.

## Tier 4 — Civil society / civic / academic

- **IndiaWaterPortal** (Arghyam, since 2007) — single largest knowledge archive for Indian water. Heavily used in NGO + academic + journalism circles.
- **SANDRP** (sandrp.in) — South Asia Network on Dams Rivers People. Independent dam + river database. Reference for activists + journalists.
- **WELL Labs** (welllabs.org) — open-data dashboards on basins, applied research. Emerging civic layer. RM partner.
- **Veditum** (veditum.org) — river archive, journalism, walks. Cultural archive of disappearing rivers.
- **Centre for Science and Environment / Down to Earth** — water reporting + reports + State of India's Environment annual.
- **Wetlands International South Asia** — wetland inventories, restoration cases.
- **ATREE** — biodiversity + water research, Eastern Himalaya + Western Ghats focus.
- **ACWADAM** — aquifer mapping reports + Bhujal Jankar (community hydrogeologist) program.
- **CEEW** — water-energy-policy reports.
- **CWAS at CEPT** — urban water + sanitation research.
- **Climate Risk Horizons** — climate-water risk reports.
- **The Third Pole** — Himalayan + cross-border water journalism.
- **Mongabay India** — environment + water reporting.
- **Praja, Janaagraha** — open data on urban governance, water-relevant.
- **2030 Water Resources Group India** — corporate-water reports.
- **Sikkim Spring Atlas** — state-level. Reference model for replication.
- **Meghalaya Springshed Management Mission** — state implementation.

**Usage read:** IndiaWaterPortal is heavy in research/NGO. SANDRP is heavy in activist/journalism. WELL Labs is growing. ACWADAM training materials reach hundreds of community hydrogeologists; should reach lakhs. The Third Pole + Mongabay shape elite + diaspora opinion.

## Tier 5 — Citizen / community

- **eBird India + waterbird counts** — wetland health proxy.
- **Friends of Lakes** (Bengaluru) — citizen lake monitoring.
- **Powai Lake watchers, Sankey Tank citizens, Hussain Sagar civic groups.**
- **Earth Watch** — citizen water testing in some regions.
- A few city-specific rainfall apps (low coverage).

**Usage read:** Local. Hobbyist-grade. Almost no national integration. The biggest gap to fill in Tier 5.

## Tier 6 — Books / canon

- **Dying Wisdom** (Anil Agarwal + Sunita Narain, CSE 1997) — historical water systems of India. Foundational.
- **Aaj Bhi Khare Hain Talab** (Anupam Mishra, 1994) — traditional rainwater harvesting. The classic.
- **A Republic of Rivers** — Mishra.
- **The Wells of Memory** (Mridula Ramesh, 2024) — recent survey.
- **Water Wars** (Vandana Shiva, 2002).
- **Watershed Development** — Crispino Lobo (WOTR).
- **Cauvery, Krishna, Ganga** — basin-specific scholarly works (varied authorship).
- **Tarun Bharat Sangh** literature on Rajasthan johad revival.
- **Nadi Ganga** — river-canon books in Hindi/Bengali.

**Usage read:** Canonical in NGO + academic circles. Not embedded in policy thinking. Reading list, not curriculum.

## Tier 7 — Tools / software

- **HEC-RAS / HEC-HMS** (US Army Corps, free) — used by Indian hydrologists.
- **SWAT** (Soil Water Assessment Tool) — used widely in research, sparingly in operations.
- **MODFLOW** — groundwater modeling.
- **QGIS + GRASS** — open-source GIS.
- **InVEST** (Natural Capital Project) — ecosystem services modeling.
- **Open Foris.**

**Usage read:** Research-grade. Operational use by state water authorities is rare.

## Adoption read — how much is actually used

If we count "publicly available substantial water artefacts" — datasets, dashboards, reports, tools, books, films — there are roughly **200-500** in active circulation. Rough breakdown:

- **Heavily used in operational decisions:** ~10-20. Mostly forecasting (IMD), dam operations (CWC), satellite (Bhuvan), and the partner work of WELL Labs / ACWADAM in their niche.
- **Used in policy + research:** ~50-100. CGWB reports, Yearbooks, IndiaWaterPortal, SANDRP, CSE, CEEW reports, academic papers.
- **Sit on shelves with modest readership:** the remaining majority. Wetland Atlas, NITI CWMI (defunct), most state water reports, books, papers, dissertations, films.

The headline pattern: **artefact-to-decision conversion rate is brutally low.**

## Conversion-rate diagnostics

A few illustrative cases:

- **GRACE shows Punjab GW collapse on satellites for 15+ years.** Punjab still grows paddy. The data did not change the policy because the policy is not data-driven, it is coalition-driven.
- **CPCB has identified 311 polluted river stretches for years.** Few prosecutions, no pricing. The data exists; the enforcement architecture doesn't translate it.
- **Wetland Atlas (2011) is 13 years old.** It hasn't been updated. Wetlands continue disappearing. Nobody is tracking the loss against the baseline.
- **Sikkim's Spring Atlas is a state-level model.** Could be replicated nationally for ~₹500 cr. Hasn't been.
- **NITI Aayog's Composite Water Management Index (CWMI)** ranked states on water performance. Embarrassing rankings. Quietly discontinued. The data became inconvenient, so the artefact disappeared.
- **JJM water-quality testing data** is collected but not aggregated publicly. The contradiction with the celebrated tap-coverage KPI is invisible.

## Four dispositions toward water artefacts in India

1. **Generative agencies** (IMD, CWC, CGWB, ISRO) — produce a lot, integrate weakly with each other, optimize for own KPIs.
2. **Civic intermediaries** (IndiaWaterPortal, SANDRP, WELL Labs, CSE, Veditum, ATREE, ACWADAM) — repackage + interpret + advocate, struggle for resourcing.
3. **Enforcement vacuum** (CPCB, NGT, state PCBs) — receive data, act sporadically, captured politically.
4. **Citizen blind spot** — almost no one outside the system can see real-time water reality.

## The missing layer

The structural diagnosis: there is no **public-good information system** that stitches Tier 1-3 data, makes it legible to citizens via Tier 4-5 channels, and creates pressure on Tier 1 + judicial enforcement.

WELL Labs is the closest current attempt. IndiaWaterPortal is the longest-running. SANDRP punches above weight. Bhujal Jankar networks (via ACWADAM) create local-grade information at community scale. **Together they are <0.1% of what's needed at scale.**

The deeper point: what's missing isn't data, and it isn't artefacts. It's the institutional infrastructure that converts artefacts into decisions, and the citizen-facing interface that creates pressure for those decisions. The water-information-as-public-good doesn't yet exist.

Building this layer — explicitly across the Tier 1-5 stack, with civic intermediaries scaled up by ~10-100× — is plausibly one of the single most leveraged interventions in Indian water. See `build-plan.md`.
