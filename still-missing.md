# Still missing — what this folder has not covered

Self-audit. The folder has thirteen prose chapters now plus this audit:

- Eight foundational + extension chapters: `README`, `landscape`, `stakeholders`, `legal-vacuum`, `data-and-groundtruth` (extended with lag tax + dark data + unanswerable questions), `artefacts`, `gaps`, `funders-ecosystem` (extended with ₹ ladder + leverage map + capture risks + whitespace seeds).
- Three deepdive chapters: `funders-flow`, `gaps-deepdive`, `hard-questions` (12 structural questions).
- One reference appendix: `data-inventory` (15-category catalog with trust grades, ~120 named sources).
- One civilizational-frame chapter: `imagination` (the meta-frame on India as water civilization).

It is sharper than most internal documents on Indian water.

It is also still partial. This file catalogues 14 categorical omissions — distinct from within-topic gaps that the existing chapters already discuss — with judgment on each: **must-fill / nice-to-have / out-of-scope**, and a recommended fill order. It also names the meta-blind-spots that any folder this size has, including the risk that the prose is becoming a substitute for action.

**One chapter remains unwritten** per the README reading order: `build-plan.md` (10-year construction logic — capital structure + founding team + risk register + sequencing). It is not an omission per se — it is the planned next chapter, downstream of `hard-questions.md`'s 12 open questions and `funders-ecosystem.md`'s whitespace seeds.

The audit posture is honest: an item ranked "must-fill" is a real gap in the argument. An item ranked "nice-to-have" is genuinely lower-priority but not invisible. An item ranked "out-of-scope" is on purpose excluded.

## Must-fill items — what each chapter would look like

### 1. Informal water economy

The Bisleri / Kinley / Aquafina / Bailley / private-borewell-vendor / RO-mechanic stack. The single largest unbanked private water flow in India, mediating water for an estimated 20-30% of urban Indians and a growing share of peri-urban + rural. Annual market plausibly ₹50,000-100,000 cr (packaged + tanker + private RO + delivery), almost entirely outside any water-as-public-good frame.

A chapter would cover: market structure (national packaged-water brands, regional packaged players, ~10 lakh local water-can operators, ~2 lakh tanker operators in major cities, RO mechanic + filter + service economy); pricing (paying 10-50× the municipal tap rate at the bottom; the urban-poor water-tax); regulation gap (FSSAI for packaged but minimal for tankers + RO + local cans); ownership concentration (Tata-Bisleri Inc., Bailley Parle, regional families); employment (largely informal, contractual, often Dalit + migrant labour); supply-chain (where the water actually comes from — borewells, municipal tap diversion, upstream tanker fleets); environmental footprint (plastic + groundwater extraction concentrations); the political-economy of who benefits from the parallel system (utilities offload service-quality failure to private vendors, who offload to consumers). Cross-references: `stakeholders.md` (urban informal as structurally absent), `funders-flow.md` (impact-failure post-mortems).

Slot in reading order: between `stakeholders.md` and `legal-vacuum.md`.

### 2. Urban water utilities

Bengaluru Water Supply and Sewerage Board (BWSSB), Delhi Jal Board (DJB), Hyderabad Metropolitan Water Supply and Sewerage Board (HMWSSB), Kolkata Municipal Corporation, Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC), Chennai Metropolitan Water (CMWSSB), Pune Municipal Corporation (PMC), Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation, Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation, Jaipur Vidyut Vitaran Nigam-style state utilities. Where the majority of urban Indians meet water — and where the gap between scheme dashboards and lived experience is widest.

A chapter would cover: governance structure (state-controlled, mayor-irrelevant); tariff politics (BWSSB tariff rises politically blocked since 2014; DJB free water up to 20 KL); non-revenue water (NRW) at 30-50% across major utilities, including theft + leakage + meter inaccuracy + administrative loss; metering (incomplete; smart-metering pilots stalled); sewage treatment (city-wise failure rates — Mumbai 100% capacity, Delhi 60%; STPs operating at <50% design capacity per CAG); coverage politics (intermittent supply; tanker dependency; urban informal denial); financing (state subsidy + AMRUT + JJM(Urban) + ADB/JICA loans); reform attempts (corporatisation, performance contracts, PPPs — all politically blocked); civic engagement (Praja, Janaagraha, Friends of Lakes, citizen ombudsmen). Cross-references: `landscape.md` Day Zero stress front; `gaps-deepdive.md` Layer 4 access.

Slot: dedicated chapter between `data-and-groundtruth.md` and `artefacts.md`.

### 3. Agriculture demand-side

The only durable answer to groundwater collapse. Three sub-layers: irrigation efficiency (drip + sprinkler + precision); cropping pattern (millets + pulses + oilseeds replacing paddy + sugar in stressed regions); farming systems (regenerative + ZBNF + organic + agroecology with reduced water demand).

A chapter would cover: scheme + market state of play (PDMC adoption, PMKSY-WDC watershed work, FPO ecosystem, ZBNF state programmes especially AP + Karnataka); economic case (drip ROI in 2-3 years for paying farmers; millets parity with rice when MSP and procurement extend); political-economy lock (Punjab paddy-electricity, Maharashtra sugarcane-Marathwada, Tamil Nadu deeper-borewell subsidy; reforming requires breaking the procurement-electoral coalition); farmer organisations driving change (Sahaja Samrudha, Watershed Support Services, Ankur, Deccan Development Society millet-revival, AP Community Managed Natural Farming); supporting NGOs (FES, WOTR, BAIF, PRADAN, Pradan, ICRISAT, MS Swaminathan Research Foundation); the Punjab transition challenge specifically (a 5-10 year programme to transition paddy belt to water-efficient cropping with full income protection); water-pricing as enabler. Cross-references: `funders-flow.md` (NGO backbone); dashboard SHEDS (Punjab + Bundelkhand).

Slot: dedicated chapter, likely after `gaps-deepdive.md` and before `imagination.md`.

### 4. Water-energy-food nexus

The single tightest political-economy triangle in Indian water. Free agricultural electricity → unmetered groundwater pumping → groundwater depletion → MSP-paddy-procurement → political coalition. The triangle is operationally one system; institutionally three siloed ministries (Power + Water + Agriculture) with separate data + budgets.

A chapter would cover: the operational coupling (Punjab + Haryana + Western UP paddy belt as the canonical case; Marathwada sugarcane; Tamil Nadu paddy belt; Bihar UP belt); the data picture (electricity consumption as proxy for pumping; CGWB block classification; APMC procurement data); modelling (SWAT + MODFLOW + agricultural-economic models exist; integrated nexus models do not, operationally); reform options (electricity feeder separation + DBT; crop-specific MSP design; procurement geographic targeting; aquifer-based water rights); state-level efforts (Punjab Pani Bachao Paise Kamao DBT pilot; Telangana Rythu Bandhu farmer income support; Andhra Pradesh natural farming); the PM-KUSUM solar-pump risk (could either reduce grid burden + enable demand-side or accelerate depletion if poorly designed). Cross-references: dashboard SHED Punjab-Haryana; `gaps-deepdive.md` Layers 5 + 6.

Slot: dedicated chapter, alongside agriculture-demand-side.

### 5. Transboundary deep-dive

The international legal layer is sketched in `legal-vacuum.md` and not developed. Each major treaty needs its own treatment.

A chapter would cover: **Indus Waters Treaty (1960)**, suspended September 2025 post-Pahalgam — the legal frame is broken, the climate frame was already broken (glaciers-fed rivers under stationarity assumptions); India's options post-suspension; Pakistan's downstream agriculture; World Bank position. **Ganga Treaty (1996)** — Farakka Barrage water sharing with Bangladesh; lean-season minimums; periodic stress. **Mahakali Treaty (1996)** — India + Nepal; Pancheshwar dam dispute, decades of stalled implementation. **Teesta** — India + Bangladesh negotiations stalled since 2011 due to West Bengal opposition; lean-season flow has fallen; Bangladesh agriculture suffers. **Brahmaputra** — India + China have no treaty; China is building large Yarlung Tsangpo dams; India + Bangladesh downstream; China shares hydrological data only seasonally; data is leveraged. Climate is rewriting all five; no climate-renegotiation clause in any. The chapter would also cover transboundary aquifers (Indus tributary aquifer; Ganga-Brahmaputra aquifer with Bangladesh) and trans-Himalayan glacial lake risk that affects multiple riparians. Cross-references: `legal-vacuum.md`, `landscape.md` cryosphere.

Slot: dedicated chapter, after `legal-vacuum.md`.

### 6. Specific basin profiles

The dashboard SHEDS array treats landscape/bioregion profiles. The Water/ folder needs basin-equivalents at SHED depth — basins not landscapes. Five priority basins: Cauvery (KA-TN-KL-PY, post-2018 SC framework), Krishna (MH-KA-AP-TG, KWDT-II + post-2014 reopening), Ganga main stem + tributaries, Yamuna (multi-state, no full tribunal despite 6 states + Delhi), Brahmaputra (no transboundary treaty; flood + arsenic + iron stack).

Each basin profile would cover: hydrology + cryosphere (where applicable); current allocation framework (tribunal awards + treaties + amendments); operational state (CWMA-equivalents; reservoir storage; flow status; water-quality status; sediment); the upstream-downstream political economy; population + agriculture; cities + utility presence; climate projections + scenario implications; existing partner + RM ecosystem coverage; gaps + opportunities. Cross-references: dashboard SHEDS array (parallel landscape geography); `landscape.md` (river health collapse stress front).

Slot: a `basin-profiles/` sub-folder with five files, OR a single combined chapter with sections per basin. Recommend the latter for first iteration; promote to sub-folder when basin profiles become operationally consequential.

### 7. Tech-stack-for-water

The construction-side question for `build-plan.md`. `gaps-deepdive.md` Layer 2 + 4 gesture at India-Stack patterns; this deserves a dedicated chapter on the actual architecture.

A chapter would cover: India-Stack components and their water analogues (Aadhaar → unique water-asset identifier; UPI → water-data consent layer; DigiLocker → water-records vault; Account Aggregator → water-data-sharing fabric; ONDC → water-services open network; DEPA → water-data privacy + protection); the existing civic-tech ecosystem that could build it (iSPIRT volunteer architects; ONDC ecosystem builders; UPI alumni + NPCI-extension talent; Microsoft Research India; Janaagraha civic-tech; Praja transparency tools; Glific + Vakt + Gram Vaani vernacular distribution); existing fragments that could federate (India-WRIS as aggregator; NWIC as proto-stewardship body; ISRO Bhuvan as satellite layer; state WRIS layers); the design principle (federated standards over centralised platform; consent-driven sharing; open APIs; vernacular-first); the build sequence (schema first; reference implementation in one basin; multi-basin federation; consumer experience built on top); the institutional vehicle (a Water Stack Foundation, Section 8, with multi-funder governance; iSPIRT + RBI + UIDAI playbook for governance design); the open-source posture (MIT or Apache 2; commercial co-existence). Cross-references: `gaps-deepdive.md` Layers 2 + 4; `funders-flow.md` diaspora-capital channel.

Slot: dedicated chapter, between `gaps-deepdive.md` and `build-plan.md`.

## Nice-to-have items — what each would look like (briefer)

### 9. Wastewater reuse and circular water economy

Singapore NEWater + Israeli wastewater reuse + Australian advanced treatment as comparators. Indian path: Bengaluru Cubbon Park reuse pilot; Chennai NEWater-style lake recharge (politically blocked); Surat industrial wastewater reuse (commercially viable); Yamuna-Delhi reuse possibility; faecal sludge management economics. Importance: matters for urban water sustainability; not primary leverage in current political-economy.

### 10. Insurance and risk pricing

Parametric crop insurance failures (PMFBY); pilot parametric water-stress insurance (a few Karnataka + AP pilots); IRDAI frameworks; ICRR-style climate-risk repositories; reinsurance industry water-risk modelling (mostly proprietary); property + real-estate water-risk pricing (essentially nascent — Bengaluru apartments still being built where water doesn't reach). Adjacent to Layer 6.

### 11. Sand mining and riverbed economy

The construction-supply-chain coupling. ~₹40,000-50,000 cr/year market. NGT + state mining-policy interventions. Sand-mining journalist deaths (Sandeep Kothari 2015 + others). Riverbed degradation as upstream-downstream environmental harm. Connected to water but adjacent.

### 12. Manual scavenging and sanitation-worker political economy

Safai Karmachari Andolan; Bezwada Wilson; Rashtriya Garima Abhiyan; manual-scavenger fatalities (hundreds annually, almost entirely Dalit). The body that carries the system. Mentioned in `stakeholders.md`; deserves deeper treatment in a water-justice chapter.

### 13. Disaster early warning

The IMD success vs other agencies' failures. Cyclone forecasting (1999 Odisha super-cyclone ~10,000 dead → recent comparable storms <100). Flood prediction less mature; GLOF early warning paper-thin (South Lhonak 2023). Instructive for Layer 1 + 4: where political cost of error is acute, Indian state builds; where it isn't, doesn't.

### 14. Children, schools, and curriculum

The Layer 7 substrate. NCERT EVS + state-board water content; eco-club networks (Paryavaran Mitra, others); Pratham + Akanksha + Eklavya + Avehi-Abacus pedagogical work; existing curriculum projects (CSE Down to Earth schools programme; ATREE Eastern Himalaya; Wipro Foundation curriculum work). Will be a sub-section of `imagination.md` when written.

## Out-of-scope items (deliberately excluded)

- **Comprehensive academic survey of Indian water.** There are 200+ books on Indian water; this folder is not one. We cite the canon, do not replicate it.
- **Quantitative basin-level hydrology + climate modelling.** Belongs in operational research; the folder argues for the institutional need without doing the modelling.
- **Per-partner research at the level of `partners.js` + `artefacts.js`.** The dashboard side covers that.
- **Comprehensive funding-database build.** A full funder-by-funder annual-report scrape would be useful but is a research artifact, not a strategy document.
- **Per-state policy comparison.** State-level water policies vary widely; an exhaustive comparison is a different document.

## Meta-blind-spots — the audit on the audit

Three honest observations about the folder itself:

**1. The prose is starting to substitute for action.** Ten chapters at ~250KB cumulative is an internal-document size that risks becoming the work, rather than the prelude to the work. The Water/README migration policy ("when this folder's thinking gets sharp enough to influence the live dashboard") was designed precisely to avoid this trap. Sharpness has to be measured by what gets shipped downstream, not by chapter count.

**2. The folder has no failure-case planning.** What happens if the build sketched in `build-plan.md` doesn't get funded? What happens if a Phase 0 pilot fails? What happens if the political-economy lock on free electricity holds for another 10 years? The chapters argue for a build; they have not done the recovery-posture work. `hard-questions.md` should address this; until it does, the folder reads as more confident than the underlying picture warrants.

**3. The folder operates within a particular epistemic register — "internal strategy material," prose-first, opinionated, English.** That register reaches a specific reader (founder, strategist, philanthropist, Rainmatter-adjacent). It does not reach the people who carry the costs of Indian water (`stakeholders.md`). A folder *for* the structurally absent would look different — vernacular, oral, narrative, rooted in lived experience. The current folder is *about* them, not *for* them. This is a category boundary; the folder can be sharp within its register and still be limited by it. **Translating the argument across registers is a separate, harder, more important project that the folder has not begun.**

## What this audit does not cover

- **The opportunity-cost question.** Is continuing to deepen the Water/ folder the right next step, or is shipping a Phase 0 pilot from `build-plan.md` more leveraged? This audit takes deepening as the question; the meta-question is contestable.
- **The competing-priorities question.** Rainmatter has multiple thesis areas; the user has multiple commitments; this folder is one of many. The opportunity-cost across the user's full portfolio is not addressed.
- **The collaboration question.** Does this folder benefit from co-authorship with one of the implementing NGOs (FES, ACWADAM, WELL Labs)? The current draft is single-author; that constrains sharpness on operational details.
- **The reader-test.** Whom should this folder be shown to before further work? A handful of trusted readers (Mihir Shah; Aditi Mukherji; Veena Srinivasan; Himanshu Thakkar; Rohini Nilekani; Amit Chandra; ATECF leadership; WELL Labs leadership; Rainmatter Foundation team) would each shift the document.
